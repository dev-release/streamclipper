StreamClipper

StreamClipper turns a multi-hour game stream recording into short vertical clips, writes a caption for each one, and publishes the clips its owner approves to YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Discord.

It runs entirely on the operator's own computer. A recording never leaves that machine — only the finished clips the operator chooses to publish do.

What it does

Finds the moments

Detects loudness peaks, transcribes the speech around them, and keeps only the moments that make sense on their own.

Builds the vertical frame

Locates the webcam window automatically, places it above the gameplay and follows the action so the important part stays in frame.

Adds captions

Word-synced subtitles in the streamer's language, with an English line underneath for viewers outside the country.

Writes the text

A title, a hook, a description and hashtags for every clip — in the streamer's own voice, in two languages.

Checks the result

Every clip is verified before delivery: silence, black frames, missing camera or a cut-off word mean the clip is rejected, not sent.

Publishes on approval

Clips arrive in Telegram for review. Nothing is published until the owner presses the button for that specific clip.

Who it is for

A single streamer — the owner of the connected accounts. StreamClipper has no sign-up, no other users, and processes no one else's recordings. The how it works page describes the full path a recording takes, and the FAQ answers the questions that come up most often.

Platform integrations

PlatformWhat StreamClipper does
YouTubeUploads an approved clip as a Short, with the title and description shown to the owner beforehand.
TikTokUploads an approved clip through the Content Posting API, after a confirmation screen with the account name, privacy level and content disclosure.
DiscordPosts an approved clip into a channel the owner chose, through a webhook.

The integration only writes. StreamClipper does not read videos, followers, comments or analytics from any platform.
