Features

Everything StreamClipper does between a raw recording and a clip ready to publish.

Finding the moments

A three-hour recording is scanned for loudness peaks — the places where the streamer reacts. Speech around each peak is transcribed locally, and the transcript is used to decide which moments hold together without the rest of the stream. A moment that only makes sense to someone who watched for an hour is discarded.

Each candidate is scored for how understandable it is on its own. Anything below the threshold never becomes a clip.

Vertical framing

Short-form video puts the streamer's webcam above the gameplay. StreamClipper finds that webcam window by itself, because its position changes between scenes and games:

  • the face is detected inside the frame, and the still background around it marks the edges of the camera window
  • a layout confirmed on one clip is reused on the others, so a clip where the streamer turned away is not left without a camera
  • if the camera moved to another corner between scenes, that is detected and the frame follows
  • the nickname bar under the camera is never cropped away

The gameplay half follows the action instead of sitting in a fixed centre crop, and holds still between movements so the picture does not drift.

Captions

Whisper returns the timing of every word, so captions are word-synced: a few words on screen at a time, the one being spoken highlighted. A translated line sits underneath for viewers who do not speak the streamer's language. Both lines carry an outline and a shadow, because plain text disappears against a bright wall or an explosion.

Sound

Clips are normalised to the loudness level short-form platforms expect. The limiter is deliberately kept shallow: pushed harder, it squashes the streamer's own microphone — the loudest source — and voices from the party chat end up louder than the streamer.

Text for publishing

Every clip gets a title, a one-line hook, a description and hashtags, in the streamer's own voice and in two languages. The text is written from what is actually said inside that clip and from a frame of it, not from a summary.

Verification

Before a clip is delivered it is checked for silence, black frames, a missing camera band, a wrong aspect ratio, a cut-off word and a weak opening. A clip that fails is not sent — the reason is reported instead.

Editing

Clip boundaries can be adjusted in plain language: shift the start, trim the end, or begin at a specific phrase. The instruction is interpreted, but the arithmetic and the search through the transcript are done exactly, so the same request always produces the same result. The title and description are kept unless changing them was requested.
